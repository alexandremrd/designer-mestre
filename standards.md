# Universal standards (U1–U13)

Single source of the standards that the modules cite by `U#` (formerly §5 of `SKILL.md`). The client's KV wins. When composing or reviewing a piece, read and apply only the `U#` it uses.

## 5. Universal standards (single source: modules cite `U#`; KV wins)
- **U1 Legibility:** preserve the technical accessibility criteria in the products they apply to, especially interactive interfaces: contrast, focus, web body text, and touch target per the pertinent norm/standard. In ads, posters, and graphic compositions do not impose a universal floor of pixels or points; derive size from the format, physical scale, distance, and reading context, and check the appropriate proof. WCAG contrast serves as a measure of sRGB pairs when relevant; it does not by itself certify a printed piece.
- **U2 9:16 safe zone:** the top (250 px) is inviolable for text and button; the bottom (340 px, 450 in Reels) comes second: with many elements the content may occupy that strip instead of squeezing, and it receives the small, discreet logo for constant brand presence; sides of 80–90 px. The safe zone is a guide for legibility, not a crop: only text, buttons, logo and key information stay inside it; background, photo, product image and decorative elements may cross it and bleed to the edge, never cut at its line. Reference: `references\safe_zones\` (Reels/Stories advertiser guide; the client's own zone goes in the client card, `Reels safe zone`). Static pieces for social networks include Feed 1080 × 1350 and Stories 1080 × 1920, unless the user explicitly says otherwise. Adapt the composition for each aspect ratio, preserving concept, copy, and identity and respecting the safe areas. Check and deliver both before declaring the delivery complete.
- **U3 Typography:** hierarchy and reading must work at the size of use; the number of families, weights, tracking, alignment, and breaks depends on the KV and the idea. The technical values in the modules are references to resolve legibility, not style quotas. Confirm the font/weight actually rendered, preserve the copy, and avoid accidental orphans; editorial composition, display, text at scale, and typographic contrast are valid tools. A requested font (KV, briefing, or user) that is not installed nor in the client's or project's folder: ask first, for each font, whether it may be installed; with the yes, research the official source (Google Fonts, foundry site, project repository), download only the free or licensed version, install for the user (without admin), and use the exact family and weight name. Paid font or uncertain license: ask the user for the file. Never swap for a fallback silently, because the piece goes out of the KV without anyone noticing. A deck follows the content, medium, and presentation context described in `html.md` §8, with no universal scale or dimensions.
- **U4 Color and surface:** the KV, the message, and contrast determine palette and proportion; do not impose a percentage of neutrals/accent, a universal limit of uses, neutrality, a surface ladder, or light/dark mode outside an interface. Intense color, gradient, texture, shadow, and flat surfaces are valid when they have function and identity.
- **U5 Space and test:** the 4/8 grid (`4 8 12 16 24 32 48 64 96`) is a useful reference, not a mandatory grid. Test viewports 375 · 768 · 1024 · 1440 when the deliverable is web.
- **U6 Distinctiveness (not an aesthetic blacklist):** a color, gradient, emoji, card, shadow, typography, or popular device is not rejected in itself. Question choices that look like generator defaults, have no role in this piece, or contradict the KV. Use the brand system as raw material and boundary, not as an obligation to repeat a composition; allow an authorial gesture when it strengthens the idea.
- **U7 Visual review:** check reading and fidelity, but also whether the piece has an idea of its own, an intentional artwork choice, and does not look like an interchangeable template. Fix concrete visual problems without reducing every direction to minimalism or a checklist; do not generate grades or reports without a request. A copy/KV violation or unproven factual claim requires correction.
- **U8 Final board:** 1 board (cuts, overlap, accents, logo, safe zone, copy). Video: 3 frames + duration + size.
- **U9 Copy ↔ visual:** understand subject, promise, and consequence so the visual converses with the copy, without being obliged to illustrate it literally. Visual metaphor, tension, contrast, expressive typography, staging, or abstraction may translate an idea; do not present them as factual proof. In a documentary scene, comparison, or diagram, use only authorized evidence. Avoid a generic image that merely names the category; text and visual must add meaning without contradicting the promise, the audience, or the KV.
- **U10 Visual series:** in a carousel/campaign, define before generation a temporal and environmental context coherent across scenes (approximate time, season/weather, and light logic), derived from the request, references, or KV; vary framing, setting, and intensity without uniformizing all photos. Each piece must add visual information to the sequence. Reusing the same image, a nearly identical crop, or an equivalent composition across different cards is rejected, unless repetition is expressly requested by the user. Check side by side on the board before delivering.
- **U11 Reference subordinate to KV:** the client's approved decisions — palette, typography, photography/light/grid, effects, graphics, and composition grammar — are the visual contract. An external reference supplies compatible mechanisms, not a complete direction that replaces that contract; ignorance is not authorization to import codes. Analysis and translation belong to `ads.md` §2; creation modules consume the result, apply only the compatible techniques, and validate the render against the KV. A change of identity requires an explicit user request.

- **U12 Product and 360 Character:** prioritize originals and official/reliable references of the same product, version, or person; AI-generated images carry less weight, even if approved, and neither replace nor contradict those sources. After consulting the local collection and the pertinent official sources, generate with AI the missing views from the best existing images. Check identity and consistency, present the board, and iterate through the creatives' approval cycle (`delivery.md` §2). Mark synthetic views as `_gerada` and record inferences and status. Only incorporate approved views into the reusable package, as synthetic support. The lack of a photo of the angle, by itself, does not block generation; visual approval does not prove inferred details. Several products or characters: one per round, because an error generated in a batch repeats in all and multiplies the cost. A correction that holds for the brand goes to the client's `design_system.md`, written as a universal textual rule, and enters the next ones before generating them. An image of another item only serves as support after confirming that the two are similar in shape and components; otherwise, the generator merges or deforms the products. **Execution:** how to call the generator (identical base prompt, one call per view, role of each reference, moving check) is in `image.md` Part E. **Engines:** external platforms and APIs (Scenario, Higgsfield/Seedance, Kling, Runway, Meshy, and similar) are options from the list in `operations.md` §0.6, never a fixed route; failed or no access = Failure Rule, and the offer includes a free local engine chosen by `local_engine.py detect`, installed only with approval. Authorizations for a real person, briefing restrictions, and cost rules still apply.
- **U13 Maximum quality and GPU first:** (1) **know the machine:** `python assets\gpu_acceleration.py profile` (`--json` for another script) returns GPU, VRAM, RAM, NVENC, and the **tier** (no GPU, basic below 6 GB, medium from 6 to 12 GB, high from 12 GB) with the recommended choice for encode, transcription, and browser; `local_engine.py detect` chooses the image model the VRAM can handle. (2) **every processing or quality task** (transcription, generation, image upscale and cutout, video render and encode, Chromium and WebGL, 3D, supersampling) uses the GPU and **the highest tier the profile sustains**, never the minimum by habit: larger transcription model, high-quality encode, browser acceleration. (3) **GPU failure or insufficient VRAM:** the script falls back to the CPU on its own, **states the reason in 1 line** (`[whisper] cuda failed (...)`) and, if the wait is long, warns; a silent fallback is a defect. (4) **confirm the actual use** (encoder log, `nvidia-smi`, line `[whisper] model X on cuda`), do not assume. (5) **large model not installed:** never download without the user's yes; offer it with the size and the gain (`operations.md` §0.6). (6) a tool without a GPU path is a declared exception in `assets\CATALOG.md`; a new script or engine ships with the GPU path implemented and the CUDA DLLs found by `gpu_acceleration.preparar_cuda()`.
