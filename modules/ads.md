# Ads module · advertising pieces and references: from concept to final file (one studio)

Routes `ads`, `ref`, `reference`, `moodboard`, `analyze`, `qa`, `creative`. **One flow for everything that is an advertising or social piece**: ad, post, story, carousel, banner, poster and key visual; the decoding of references that guides creation; and the analysis or adjustment of a creative that already exists. What changes between requests is the **entry profile** (§0). This file replaces the former `referencias.md`; nothing from it was left out. Delivery cycle: `delivery.md` §2. Keep the preview workbench separate from the final file: use a working file suited to creation and only export to `Exportados\` after approval and format choice; follow the folder or destination the user indicates.

## 1. Advertising art direction and ideation (`Insight → Tension → Visual Proposition`)
- **Orchestration of competencies (do not load everything):** this module owns the proposition, the concept, the composition and the applied copy. `image.md` Part C directs or selects the photography when the image takes part in the idea, even if the asset comes from the library; `quality.md` §§A and H run the independent gate after the render; a long briefing calls `briefing.md` first and returns to this route; generating a scene calls the `image.md` engine; assembling in Figma only after a request or approval calls `figma.md`. Do not trigger video, HTML or UI, product 360 or all the skills without the corresponding task. Each specialist receives the result and the limits of the previous step; do not hand the ad's composition over to the image module nor duplicate the reference pipeline in it.
- **Creation guidance:** use the message, the KV, the assets and the references to start composing. Proposition, function of the elements, hierarchy, visual grammar and origins per layer are useful direction questions when they clarify the idea, not a form to fill before opening the canvas. Adjectives inspire exploration, but the piece makes them concrete in visual choices; when the KV leaves room open, explore a compatible solution instead of freezing the layer or importing a style without intent.
- **Idea method (concept from scratch):** short inventory of what only this client has, the sentence "in this piece, [something from the subject] becomes [the visual resource]" and the competitor test (put their name in the sentence; if it still works, the idea belongs to the category). It is not a form to fill before the canvas and in `/fast` it does not run. Detail in `html.md` §3.
- **Five questions per piece, section or scene** (internal check, without stopping to ask; in `/fast` skip): narrative role (opens, transition, data, quote, closes); observer distance (phone at 10 cm, notebook, projection); visual temperature (calm, tense, authoritative, affectionate); capacity (does the content fit? sketch 3 thumbnails); visual motive (the element or metaphor only this subject has; with no answer, you are drawing lots for a style). Source: `references\huashu\workflow.md`.
- **Appearance memory:** before a new piece, read the last lines of the client's `historico.md`; the recent visual family indicates what to vary (U10). When recording the delivery, append `| aparência: <três palavras>` to the line.
- **Visual intent:** for copy with a concrete commercial claim, identify what it claims and choose a scene or composition that supports it without inventing proof. For simple or informative content, it is enough to define the main message and the function of the visual; do not force a causal chain.
- **Advertising objective, not a performance promise:** identify audience and context, objective of the piece, authorized benefit or tension, available evidence and next action. With enough information, articulate problem or situation, the brand's real advantage, proposition, proof and action; treat audience insight without evidence as a hypothesis, never as fact. Build the path attention, understanding or reason to believe, action suited to the briefing; the hook grabs attention but does not replace proposition or proof. Without confirmed differentiator or proof, use only what the sources support and record the gap. **Never invent urgency, scarcity, testimonial, number or result.** Without comparable campaign data, evaluate clarity, relevance, credibility and ease of acting as design criteria; do not claim the piece will convert or sell.
- **Commercial offer (only when the briefing brings a promotion or condition):** identify the offer mechanism, the audience or objection it addresses, confirmed deadline or urgency and the price, margin, stock and eligibility restrictions provided. Separate facts from hypotheses; no commercial condition or scarcity may be invented. In a sales series, vary mechanism, angle, format or funnel role when the differences are relevant, instead of swapping only color or finish. KPI is an evaluation hypothesis, not a forecast, and a specific metric is only indicated when objective and comparison context are known.
- **Idea, campaign and execution:** do not confuse a visual resource, format or channel with the advertising idea. The execution materializes a proposition; the campaign platform can guide several pieces, but does not become lasting brand positioning. A one-off piece does not reinvent the brand strategy; a campaign makes explicit what stays and what varies.
- **Handoff between capabilities:** when the image helps, trigger `image.md` Part C with the intent, context and factual limits already known; do not require a closed technical briefing before opening the workbench. Iterate between layout and image when the relationship between them is being discovered. Preserve person and product and do not fabricate factual proof.
- **Choice of visual support:** real image, generated image, graphic device, typography or a mix can star in the piece; combine or change the roles of these elements as the composition evolves, without forcing the photo, the packshot or a specific asset to explain the copy alone. Respect provenance, authorization and facts.
- **Mood as observable behavior:** translate abstract words into verifiable visual decisions (typography invading the margin, abrupt chromatic contrast, monumental frontal portrait, editorial silence). These are examples of distinct registers: choose what gives body to the concept, without using any one as the default style.
- **New concept, from scratch:** find a strong visual direction and start building; let the form emerge from the concept and the feedback, without a fixed sequence of hypotheses or a form that blocks progress. Explore alternatives when they help avoid a cliché or when the user asks (metaphor, contrast, demonstration, typographic or editorial gesture, narrative, scale are possibilities, not mandatory categories). The direction can be exuberant, cinematic, typographic, editorial, minimalist or hybrid, according to the idea and the KV. In `/fast`, go straight ahead with a direction of your own. The format ("quote card", "poster", "carousel") informs the context, it does not impose a template.
- **Concept, variations and directions:** choose a visual territory that makes the message clearer and more specific to the brand. Generate several visible directions only when the user asks for options or when a real ambiguity changes the solution; the internal drawing of directions (`draw.mjs`, §3) is an agent tool and does not show three pieces to the user. When variations are requested, differentiate the directions by **idea and composition**, not only by color or effect; the method of generating three directions that differ in structure, not only in skin, is in `references\huashu\directions.md` (file in Chinese; read it for its content and do not adopt the fixed gate of stopping at each piece).
- **Heroic, advertising or cinematic direction:** it is a strong creative direction, not a last resort nor reserved for hero products or launches. Consider narrative, scale, gesture, perspective, light and atmosphere also in executive, educational or quote content, when that gives form to the idea and respects the KV; combine or dispense with these resources according to the concept, without reducing it to sunset, flare, mist or effects.
- **Poster and key visual:** treat the canvas as a composed field, not as a photo with a title added. Define the reading distance and context; test who dominates in thumbnail and whether the necessary information is recoverable at reading size. Choose the type and image relationship (contrast, integration, interruption, repetition or other) by content and KV. Distance, typographic levels, density, overlap and texture vary by piece; do not impose a block count, palette, grid, worn treatment or fixed "cinema" aesthetic.
- **Cinema in a still image:** direct a legible narrative instant (action or tension, subject position and scale, relationship with the environment, gesture and gaze, forces that guide the reading) when the idea calls for narrative. Camera, perspective, light, color, depth, grain and poster format are consequences of the scene and the KV. Do not convert the Rule of Six, lens formulas or cinema language into a checklist for a static ad; movement, coverage and shot rhythm belong to `video.md`.
- **Series and campaign (P2):** before composing several pieces, mark the **invariants** that give recognition (color and type roles, signature, compatible treatment, logo behavior) and the **variables** that advance message and rhythm (action, scale, scene, distance, crop, density). Do not freeze all pieces into a repeated frame nor introduce a new visual grammar without a role. Compare the renders side by side and confirm that each variation fulfills a real function, with the exact copy (U10).
- **Concept in motion:** for video or UGC, one possible progression is an opening that interrupts scrolling, authorized action or proof, payoff or reaction, final action; the number and duration of beats follow the story and the platform (`video.md` §2.1 and §5). Do not require this sequence in a still image nor fabricate proof to complete a beat.
- **Template is not an automatic starting point:** only clone a template when the user asks for continuity, the piece explicitly belongs to an approved series or reuse is a requirement. In a "from scratch" request, do not clone the previous piece nor ready-made coordinates; use only relevant brand resources. An approved archetype is a reusable reference, not an instruction to clone on every request.

### 1.1 Creation engines: consultation order (`uiux` comes first)
The piece's **contract** is the client's KV (U11). Within it, the skill's engines work in **this order**, each returning candidates that the KV filters; none replaces what the KV already defines, and all fill gaps and ground choices. When the concept is new or the KV leaves room open, start with `uiux`.
| Priority | Engine | Decides | Command or file | What comes back |
|---|---|---|---|---|

| 6 | `designlang` (`engines\designlang`) | tokens and language of **a live reference site** (color, typography, scale, motion) | `html.md` §6.4 | `*-design-language.md` for the §2 decoding |

**Rules for using `uiux`:**
- **The query is in English.** The bank is in English: translate segment and tone ("joalheria elegante feminina" becomes `jewelry elegant luxury feminine`). In Portuguese it returns "No matches" or falls on a generic result (trust blue, "general use") unrelated to the segment; a generic result that does not fit the segment means a query that missed. Rephrase with English synonyms and, if it persists, declare "no data in the bank" instead of using the default silently.
- **The three dials of `--design-system`:** `--variance` (1 conservative to 10 experimental), `--motion` (1 subtle to 10 striking) and `--density` (1 airy to 10 dense). Request for a client without a complete KV: the result is a **suggestion** and the KV wins. `--persist` writes `MASTER.md`: only with `--output-dir` pointing to the delivery's `Trabalho\`, never to the skill folder.
- **Traceable:** record in the piece's `.txt` and in the `historico.md` line which engines were used (`uiux: estilo e tipografia · taste: direção 2`), so the choice can be redone or corrected.
- **Nothing is lost for lack of a trigger:** each engine in the table has its activation condition in this section; an engine capability that no flow triggers is an integration failure and goes to the pending items.

## 2. References: decode the visual language and translate to the KV
Activate when the user indicates images as reference or inspiration, asks for `/designer-mestre ref` (aliases `reference` and `moodboard`), or wants to understand the KV, the visual language or the style of a reference brand or campaign. It turns visual materials into **short sheets and transferable rules**: analyze each reference once and record a synthesis that guides creation without reopening the material. Analysis or adjustment of the client's **own** creative is §6, not this: do not confuse diagnosis of the piece with decoding of references.
### 2.1 Analysis economy and where to save
- View the references **once**, together: build a board (`_board_refs.jpg`, up to 2000 px wide, 4 to 8 per sheet) and read only the board; open a single image only if a detail cannot be read on it. Extract optics, light, grid, proportions and palette directly from the image. Save the result as **text**; after that, the agent reads only the text (never the images) to create.
- Asset sheet capped at ~700 characters; system or campaign synthesis up to 1.5 KB; client file capped at ~8 KB. Past that, merge sheets and remove redundant observations, without deleting evidence that supports a rule. No per-image color analysis by script: estimate from the board and measure with `check_image.py palette` (contrast, palette, board and image parity) only if the color becomes a token.
- **Where to save:** reference images (1024 px copies) in `[Cliente]\Compartilhada\Referências\KV_Inspiracao\[AAAA-MM]\` and the board in the same folder; sheets, conversion to the KV and idea bank in `[Cliente]\_DESIGN\referencias.md`. **A client's reference stays only in their folders**, specific to them; nothing from it enters the skill. A reference for the skill (method, technique, style) is **not stored**: it is analyzed and **translated into a universal principle** in the owning module, without the file, brand, person or work of origin, written without naming client or source; the license credit, when required, goes only in `NOTICE.md`.
### 2.2 4-step pipeline and standard sheet
Decode every reference in **5 dimensions**: *Camera* (height, angle, framing, perspective) · *Light* (direction, hardness, contrast, apparent source) · *Depth* (planes, focus, hero separation) · *Atmosphere* (color, texture, mood, density) · *Composition* (visual weight, negative space, commercial function), following **4 steps**: (1) `Physical Evidence` (what is visible); (2) `Structural Pattern` (the optical, typographic or compositional mechanics behind it); (3) `Transferable Rule to the KV` (the principle translated to the client's tokens); (4) `Anti-Rule / Veto` (what to discard to avoid plagiarism or an AI look). Record what is visible; lens, aperture, Kelvin, equipment and original prompt are hypotheses, unless verifiable metadata. If the reference does not show an axis, leave that axis free for the client's direction.
```text
### R[n] · [short nickname] · [file]
Evidence & Technique: camera/framing/perspective · visible light/direction/contrast · planes/focus · atmosphere/palette/texture · composition/hierarchy/CTA when present.
Creative eye: the visual proposition behind it (metaphor, tension, crop) in 1 sentence.
Transferable Rule in the KV [client]: what goes in (translated to the client's tokens).
Anti-Rule (Vetoes): what does NOT go in (third parties' proprietary elements or conflicts with the KV).
Use in: type of post/slide/LP where it works best.
```
### 2.3 Reverse engineering of a reference's visual system
Activate when the request is to understand the **KV of the reference brand or campaign**, not just to take a piece as inspiration. Read the indicated source and classify the object: **brand system** (durable codes across different touchpoints or campaigns), **KV or campaign platform** (recurring rules within a campaign; do not generalize to the whole brand) or **isolated piece** (observable choices in that execution; without other samples, do not declare them system rules). The same reading applies to the client's own SVG reference: read the board and the structure before composing and extract the system by **function and relationship**, not only by appearance (which shape is signature, motif, mask, content or ornament; how text, photo, color, rhythm and depth work together; what repeats or varies), preserving the function of the official identity and the literal copy. A similar shape does not authorize swapping roles.
- **Evidence trail:** file or URL, channel or campaign and available date or context. For each conclusion, separate **observed**, **inferred** and **unverifiable**. Low confidence with a single piece or uncertain context; recurrence across several pieces of a campaign supports medium confidence about that KV; high confidence about a brand code requires coherent recurrence across independent campaigns or touchpoints. Repetition within the same campaign supports a campaign-KV hypothesis, not a permanent identity one. Contradiction is variation or a gap to explain, not noise to erase.
- **Compare relationships and functions, not only colors or objects:** role and behavior of the logo; chromatic roles and contrasts; hierarchy and expressive use of typography; recurring shapes, graphic devices and textures; image and portrait treatment; construction of the composition, rhythm, density and negative space; message hierarchy and adaptation by channel. Mark each finding as **recurring code**, **campaign device**, **channel convention** or **one-off choice** and note one relevant exception.
- **Reference that is a live site:** extract the tokens and language with `designlang` (`html.md` §6.4) and read the `*-design-language.md` through the same 4-step pipeline.
- **Structure that makes a KV operational:** concrete rules organized by role (theme, semantic colors, typographic hierarchy, components, layout, depth and elevation, do's and don'ts, prompt guide). Apply the clarity of roles and restrictions, not the tokens or components of interface systems, to ads and campaign photography.
### 2.4 Transfer to a client piece
1. Read first the `design_system.md`, the relevant approved pieces and the briefing restrictions. Treat the proven KV as a **contract** for color, typography, photography, light and grid, effects, graphic elements and composition, not only as palette or logo.
2. Classify the intent of the references as **ambition**, **technique** or **explicit request for identity change**. Without the latter, no external reference revokes the KV (U11). Missing information about a client code does not turn that code into a free field.
3. **Two or more references:** choose a **main** one by compatibility with the KV and the message; name the function of each complementary one (light, depth, finish) and use its technique only in a distinct layer compatible with the KV. Several techniques can coexist; do not import a whole aesthetic nor combine conflicting palettes, typographies or grammars. The user's explicit indication guides the selection, but does not change the KV without a clear redesign request. Real product or face and the KV are not replaced by an aesthetic reference: transfer mechanisms, not the subject nor third parties' identity. Full reverse engineering of 1 photo to generate an identical scene in AI triggers the **Vision Decomposer** (`image.md` Part B §5). For each technique chosen, make explicit the adaptation to the client's codes and what was discarded.
4. Deliver an **executable direction**, not adjectives: portrait crop and position; light and grading compatible with the photo and the KV; allowed effects and graphic devices; path and masses of the composition; the client's typographic family and hierarchy; the client's palette. Each decision points to the KV source or to the technique being adapted.
5. **Preservation comparison after the render.** If photography, contrast, color, typography, effects or graphic signature came to belong to the reference instead of the client, the adaptation failed even if the piece is well finished: fix before delivering. Only change a KV code with the user's explicit request.
### 2.5 Synthesis, originality guard and use in creation
- Deliver the synthesis in four blocks: (1) **observed visual grammar** (elements, relationships and evidence that repeat); (2) **variation and limits** (what changes between pieces and channels, and the still-weak hypotheses); (3) **translation to the client's KV** (fixed codes, reference mechanism chosen for each layer, compatible adaptation, conflicting items discarded); (4) **original direction** (a specific execution that preserves the client's codes in all layers, improves the relationship between message and image and does not copy the layout; the copy stays literal, Law 4).
- In the client's `referencias.md` file, at the end: **recurring patterns** (3 to 6 lines), **recipes for the KV** (3 to 6, ready and actionable combinations) and **idea bank** (5 to 10 lines, post or slide concepts already in the KV).
- **Originality guard:** discard literal subject, third-party identity, logos, slogans, mascots, text, proprietary assets and distinctive composition; transfer only the physics of light, proportion, rhythm and finish. A third-party reference never becomes the client's KV.
- **Calibration:** an isolated poster = describe the piece and leave the broad identity as a hypothesis; several ads of a campaign = extract the KV of that campaign; the same codes across site, social and different campaigns = candidates for recurring brand codes, always citing the sources. For a single image, describe only what it allows observing and do not claim to have rebuilt a complete brand book. Reading the reference does not replace or change the validated `design_system.md`.
- **In creation with references:** cite in 1 line which sheets and functions (`R1 [Light] + R3 [Camera]`) were applied. A missing reference does not block the piece: use the initial direction from `image.md` Part C. An approved recipe goes up to `## 2. O QUE FAZER` of `design_system.md`.

## 3. Composition: idea, masses, typography and finish
- **Visual construction:** choose the engine and the workbench that give the most power to the idea, the render and the iteration, within the available tools; do not presume SVG as engine nor as output. Compose in the context of use when known; if the final format is still open, use a flexible workbench and adapt the delivery file after approval. Choose entry, path and focal points by the message; do not require a single focal point, three levels or a numbered order in every layout. A portrait can give authorship and context, but if the thesis is causal it alone does not demonstrate it (U9).
- **Solving the composition:** start from the idea and the feedback, not from a familiar layout. Use scale contrast, overlap, framing, negative space, controlled abundance, multiple focal points or any solution that serves the concept; show the work in preview and allow adjustment on the workbench, without generating separate boards or alternatives unless they help or are requested. For per-screen arrangements of your own (carousel, series), draw directions with `taste` (§1.1, priority 2: `node <AG>\assets\taste\scripts\draw.mjs --sector <sector> --options 3`) after consulting `uiux`, and adapt from viewport to canvas.
- **Composing with intent:** define alignments, breathing room and text and image relationships according to the idea and the copy; a grid can organize or be tensioned on purpose. Shapes, bars, badges, frames, numbers, texture and ornaments come in when they add language, rhythm or meaning.
- **Typographic composition:** treat headline, letters and typography as visual form when it serves the direction; use the KV font or a compatible substitute (U3), with no aesthetic prohibition of specific families. Check that the font really appears and that text, contrast and breaks work at the scale of use.
- **Art decision:** examine the reading at appropriate size and context, together with the briefing and the references. Distinction is a creative question to guide the next iteration, not a gate to reject a piece for style, popularity, structure or degree of ornamentation; identity, copy and authorized proof are the real limits.
- **Feedback and refinement:** treat the preview as art under construction: identify the perceived change and edit the workbench directly; do a broad redesign when the direction calls for it, without limiting to one mutation per cycle. Do not require thumbnail comparison nor a textual justification of each decision to the user.
- **Copy is not adapted:** the approved text goes in literal. When it does not fit, change the composition, size or line break; shortening, summarizing or rewriting is the user's decision. A conflict between copy and layout is pointed out in 1 line, not solved alone.
- **KV as identity, not a closed layout:** check that the result remains recognizable and compatible with the client's important codes and the current request. References stimulate new composition, scale or language, without copying third-party identity or altering logo, facts or protected assets. Preserve the confirmed invariants; adapt brand codes when that strengthens the idea and does not contradict them. Material conflict between request and KV: stop and ask.
- **Render review:** check the thumbnail in social pieces; use grayscale if the hierarchy depends on tonal contrast; fix ambiguous touches and check cuts and overflow on the canvas. These are checks to find real defects, not steps to fill a checklist.

## 4. Formats and choice of creation workbench
- **Static pieces for social networks:** apply U2 to produce, check and deliver the **Feed (1080×1350) + Stories (1080×1920) pair**; a single size does not conclude the delivery, except for an explicit user exception. This is different from "generate only what the user chooses" (final format, below), which concerns the file's **extension and profile**, not the pair of proportions.
- **Specifications per platform (social piece):** `references\design_skills\social-media-graphic\SKILL.md` brings the dimensions per network (Instagram, X, LinkedIn, Facebook), safe zones, trends, template structures, the carousel system and the quality checklist; **check the platform's current specification before using a value**, and the U2 Feed and Stories pair still rules.
- **Composition before engine:** `references\design_skills\visual-design\SKILL.md` is optional support for hierarchy, grid, space and reading when diagnosis is needed; it does not impose an engine nor replace the KV.
- **Static in HTML/CSS:** use an artboard at the final dimensions, local fonts and assets and stable layout; the preview scale does not alter the exported composition. HTML/CSS speeds up typography, masks and effects; direct SVG usually reduces friction in maps, diagrams and vector shapes. Creativity depends on direction and composition, not on the extension.
- **Editable export contract:** SVG is a final option, not an authoring obligation. When requested, plan texts and shapes as native SVG elements or rebuild them on export; check the render and, when possible, the import in the target editor. Do not deliver `foreignObject` nor encapsulated PNG as if they were editable vector layers. Effects without an equivalent can be rasterized in a localized and declared way, preserving editable text, logo and shapes and the HTML/CSS source. Without an SVG request, do not create a second implementation just to offer it. **SVG text:** embedded font (`@font-face`) and CSS only work in the browser. The SVG the client opens in Figma, Illustrator or Canva comes out **without `<style>`, classes or filters** (`assets\logo\svg_audit.py`), with **editable text with explicit family and weight when the target editor has the font** (confirm by reopening the file; this is the case of Route C in `figma.md`) and **in outlines when it cannot be confirmed** (`quality.md` §K). When in doubt, deliver the outlined version.
- **Workbench and preview:** choose the medium in which the piece can be seen and adjusted with the least friction. If available, use HTML, CSS and JS in the Browser canvas as an editable, iterable composition tab, with simple visual context and controls only when they help; inline SVG can be content of that canvas. The page serves to build and review, it is not automatically the final format.
- **Possible media:** HTML/CSS for a responsive or interactive visual workbench; SVG for vector drawing; native Figma when the user asks for Figma structure; PPTX for an editable PowerPoint presentation when library and environment exist; raster for photography or illustration or a chosen raster delivery; hybrid composition when each medium has a role. Choose by the nature of the artifact, the editability the user needs, the available tool and the preview and export path; do not convert everything to a single medium out of habit.
- **Final format:** differentiate the piece's proportion and destination from the export extension. Use the stated destination to guide the composition; after approval, if the format, size or output standard are neither explicit nor recorded for the client, ask before exporting. Generate only what the user chooses.
- **Common proportions (reference, not preset):** Feed 4:5, Story and Reels 9:16, social image or thumbnail in the platform's proportion, document in the physical size of the request or the printer, banner according to the application. Values and safe zones only apply when they match the destination's current specification; do not force universal `viewBox`, dimension, margin, CTA or central area.
- **Printed poster:** before the final file, confirm dimension and proportion, bleed, safe area, scale, color mode and resolution with the request or the supplier, distinguishing trim, bleed, safe area, print scale and effective DPI. Without a specification, make the piece on the workbench and ask what is missing after approval; do not invent preflight, CMYK or print-shop readiness, and do not presume 300 DPI.
- **Carousel:** keep the relationship between screens that the user asked for; proportion, canvas and cadence are chosen by the platform, the sequence and the composition, not by a universal `viewBox` nor by the same layout repeated.
- **Photography and crop:** preserve identity, product and the necessary information. Crop, mask, proportion, perspective distortion or extreme framing may be used on purpose if they do not alter product or person in a misleading way nor harm the objective. Never stretch or squash to fit.
- **Logo:** use the official asset, keep the proportion and follow the brand guide when it exists (Law 3 and `kv-logo.md` §1.1). Without specification, choose size, contrast and breathing room by the composition and the legibility at the real destination; do not impose percentages, pixel minimums or a universal light or dark color.

## 5. Preview, refinement and final export
- **Preview:** show the workbench in the Browser canvas when available and update the content while iterating; if there is no usable interactive view, present a capture or rendered image. Do not reduce the workbench to a Figma screen mock nor claim the file is Figma. A preview with provisional dimension is identified as such, without confusing it with the export size.
- **Review:** `quality.md` §§A and H are the independent gate after the render, read as **concrete critique** (copy, identity, proof, reading, craft) and not as an aesthetic gate requiring a single focus, structure or style. Fix what contradicts the request, the KV or factual integrity; an expression decision is not a defect for being unusual.
- **Image:** look for client assets and trigger `image.md` when the concept needs it; text, logo and data that must be exact come from assets or editable layers on the workbench, never from visual generation.
- **Approval:** keep the workbench in progress until the user approves or asks for direct delivery; do not generate a final export nor a definitive name for approval. After approval, confirm unspecified extension, dimension or profile, naming and destination, respecting the client's confirmed standards.
- **Export:** use the native path most faithful to the chosen format and review the exported file in that format (`quality.md` §K): fonts, assets, limits, crops, colors and legibility according to the application; rasterize only when necessary or requested. A print file follows the supplier's requirements.
- **Naming and destination:** apply the standard from `delivery.md` §7 to the next deliveries, preserving existing ones; confirm only the still-undefined destination or format. Working and temporary files stay distinct from final exports.

## 6. Analysis and adjustment of an existing creative (P3)
- For the diagnostic eye use the script of `quality.md` §H over the **render**, not over the code, and the single list of signals in §A; the order of fixing goes from the idea to the motion.
- First understand the task: **analysis** asks for diagnosis, **adjustment** asks for a one-off change, **redesign** asks for a new composition. Do not turn analysis into redesign nor rewrite a file without a request.
- Analyze the piece in the format provided (SVG is not a prerequisite): for raster, capture, PDF or render, observe the image at use size and in thumbnail when it makes sense; with SVG or XML, combine the visual reading with inspection of the structure (texts, fonts, transforms, masks and clips, groups, bounds). Do not invent structural information the file does not offer.
- Read copy, function and identity before judging. Determine what should grab attention first, the real reading path and whether image, typography and graphic devices support the message. Preserve what already works; distinguish intentional aesthetic choice from error of hierarchy, legibility, alignment, cut or consistency.
- Base each finding on evidence from the file or the render: element or ID or text excerpt, observable position or relationship and effect on reading. Prioritize **at most three changes** of greatest impact; explain the problem first and then a concrete action. Avoid empty praise, generic adjectives, numeric grades without evidence and lists of micro-adjustments that do not change the result. Distinguish visual quality from performance: without real campaign data, do not attribute results to the visual and do not adopt platform benchmarks as universal.
- Editing request: work in the available format and source, preserving identity, authorized content and useful structure; render again when there is an editable file and compare at use size and in thumbnail. If there is only a flattened image: propose a compatible edit or ask for the source only when needed to carry out the adjustment. Declare the inspection limits without turning the absence of XML into a block.
